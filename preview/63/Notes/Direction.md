# Direction

Where the game is heading, from a conversation on 1 Oct 2026. Brainstorm-grade: the rules here are settled enough to build
toward, the details are not. It does not replace the system docs; where it disagrees with one, that doc is to be revisited
(listed at the end).

## The problem this answers

The simulation became believable and stopped being a game. The old Overworld had XP, a shop and floors that could kill you:
crude, but there was something to want and something at stake. The ecology has neither. The world is huge and does almost
nothing mechanically; XP would make the game about whatever gives XP; free mutations would make it about whichever mutations
help find the next one.

The answer is not less believability. Believability is what makes danger learnable. What was missing is a want and a stake,
and both can come out of the physics.

## The shape

- **An island with a real ecology.** Normal, believable animals, the ones already built, in a space a life can cross. The
  planet is the backdrop and is still to be finished (the planet viewer gets its fair shake); the game happens on the island.
- **Muties.** A small number of animals carry the mutagen organ (below). They are the cast: single individuals, or whole lines
  branched off a species and now fairly common.
- **The player is a mutie.** Not a god, not a normal animal: something the ecology beneath it cannot sustain.
- **Not isolated.** Rumours (on land) of muties who went out over the ocean and are colonising the archipelagos. Mutation may
  not have started here at all. Where it came from is never answered; there are clues, never a statement, and nothing written
  in English.

## The rule for mutation: unexplained, never unpaid

How the organ works is a mystery. What goes in and what comes out obey the same physics as everything else. Nothing a mutie
does is free; everything it builds is an ordinary body, only one nothing else can afford. Mutation is new, not magic. This is
the core rule of `Design-Principles.md` extended, not an exception to it.

**No moving beyond the physics,** demigods included. The game's worth is that it can be reasoned about; one real exception and
the player stops reasoning and starts guessing. The awe comes from scale, not from breaking rules.

## The mutagen organ

A physical organ (or series of cells) in the body map: it has mass, costs upkeep, can be damaged, and grows slowly as the body
eats. It can live in anything.

- **It wrings the sock dry.** Real animals waste most of what they eat (undigested matter, heat, ~90% lost at each step up a
  food chain). The organ extracts nearly all of the chemical energy in a meal. Extreme, but the energy was always in the food.
  The ceiling is the energy content of what is eaten; nobody passes it.
- **The surplus is spent on body.** Editing your animal costs what the organ has made. Brains, structures, lifespan, senses:
  complexity the ecology beneath cannot pay for.
- **Jevons.** Efficiency does not make a mutie less hungry; it makes complexity affordable, so it builds more, so it eats more.
  Every mutie is hungry for a bigger body. That is the motivation engine.
- **Muties are rich food.** They carry the most mutagen, so muties are worth eating to other muties. Every meeting has that
  undertone. Politics starts there.

## The ecology is the commons

Muties are expensive. The island has a real carrying capacity and the muties draw it down. Planters, builders and hunters spend
one shared budget in different ways, and that is the politics. The normal animals become what everyone depends on and nobody
owns, and the simulation can show it honestly: who ate whom, what thinned out, which valley went quiet. The archipelago
colonists are a threat, not flavour: more mouths.

## The demigod is the apex of the energy flow

Demigod status is the most mutagen, the most mass, the nearest the efficiency ceiling: the creature the island's energy runs
into. The throne is the top of the food pyramid, literally. Its cost is visible: the ecology thins around it. To normal animals
it is a god because they cannot model it at all. Transcendence at the top is magnitude (senses across the island, deliberation
over years, structures that change local weather), never a free output.

## Muties

**A mutie is a base species' body map plus edits.** The base stays visible: a mutie carries its origin, its reproduction, the
parts of its old form that came through unchanged. Recognising the base is a skill. Sometimes it is cool, sometimes horrifying.

- *A grazer, two metres tall,* with the grazer jaw still on it. It has gone mixovore and is largely a predator now; it will eat
  you. You realise what it was a moment too late.
- *Another grazer that kept its niche,* still eats plants, but long and wide.
- *A self-replicating colony* that grew large and clever and still buds.

**Sapience is not smarts.** It is more investment in biology, performing complexity the ecology is not used to and cannot
sustain. A sapient behaviour is an animal behaviour plus foresight:

| animal does | plus foresight | gives |
|---|---|---|
| nests | plans the nest | building |
| forages fruit, drops seeds | plants deliberately | agriculture |
| shares food with kin | remembers who shared | trade, debt |
| fights for dominance | remembers individuals | politics |
| scent-marks territory | marks for others to read | something like writing |

So a mutie's character is what its body still wants plus the cognition to plan for it, read off the body map like everything
else. Each mutation solved one problem and left a hole; the hole is the want, and wants are what quests are made of.

**Cast ideas** (examples, not a list to build):

- *The sterile line.* Breeds true, cannot recombine, slowly accumulating defects. Desperate for new material: other muties,
  their mutagen, their children. Sexual against asexual reproduction is a live axis across the whole cast.
- *The non-eaters.* Muties that need almost no food and so persist for centuries; some near sessile, some still thinking. Their
  remaining drives are whatever survived. They are the archive: they remember what the island was before.
- *The planters.* Plant trees deliberately. They engineer the ecology and displace the animals in it; the quietest danger.
- *The builder.* Structures that outlast it, so old structures are ruins with a known builder.
- *A predator line that got foresight.* Ambush plus planning: traps.
- *A colonial line.* One mind across many bodies.

## Communication by channel

A mutie talks in the channels its body has: scent, colour display, sound, posture. **You can only understand a language your
body can perceive, and only speak one it can produce.** A scent-talker needs the chemoreceptors; a colour display needs the
receptors (`spectra.js` becomes a gate, not only a renderer). Each language is a capacity to grow, then a vocabulary learned by
watching. Normal animals' alarm calls and threat displays are the first, crude languages, and some talk with non-muties is
possible. The social game cannot really begin until the player has these.

Transducers reading much more complex information is the general form: small scale you see; large scale you hold more and can
deliberate more.

## Starts: capacities, not abilities

The player begins as an intelligent life form, most often the child of a mutie: a parent (first relationship, first teacher,
first loss, first goals) and a body already capable. But what is given is the capacity, not the ability:

- the organ, small;
- a hub that is capable and untrained (the existing hub mechanic: a body you have the parts of and cannot yet drive);
- one or two edits already made.

Speaking to a given animal, building, and further edits each cost mutagen to grow and practice to drive. A full kit at the
start would leave nothing to climb.

## What the player wants

- **Mutagen**, to build a bigger, stranger, more capable body, and to not be eaten for it.
- **Languages**, to reach muties and animals that are otherwise closed.
- **What other muties need**, and what they have: trade, alliance, betrayal.
- **The island's future**, since the commons is running down.
- **What happened here**, from the non-eaters, the ruins, the bodies themselves. Clues, never answers.
- **The top of the energy flow**, at whatever it costs the island.

## Open

- Whether mutation is inherited reliably, and how reproduction enters (lineage play, or one individual per run).
- Which edits exist, what each costs, and how the player chooses them.
- How a sapient mind runs. The node wiring runs reflexes; planning and memory of individuals is a far bigger problem.
  Exchanges of objects and signals before words is cheaper and truer than dialogue trees.
- How long a life is, in turns and in minutes.
- What ends a run, and what ends the game.

## Docs this moves

  the mechanism. To be reconciled with the organ as surplus spent on edits.
- `Lore.md` has a shapeshifting demigod outside the physics. This direction keeps the word and drops the exception; the person
  decides which stands.
- `Design-Principles.md` holds; this extends it to muties.
